PPC Management

Turn paid search spend into measurable inquiries

Build Google Ads campaigns around the services customers are looking for, the areas you can serve, and the inquiries your team can convert into jobs.

Discuss Your Project →All Services →

What the service covers

Campaign structure and intent

Group terms by service and intent. Use geography, ad schedules, and negative keywords to limit irrelevant searches. Match ad copy to a useful landing page.

Reliable conversion measurement

Test phone and form tracking before increasing spend. Separate raw inquiries from qualified leads, and consider importing booked or sold jobs when the data is reliable.

Budget and optimization

Review search terms, spend, and lead quality together. Shift budget toward the services and markets with evidence of demand rather than chasing the cheapest clicks.

How the work starts

  1. Audit the account, campaign goals, landing pages, and tracking.
  2. Launch or restructure priority campaigns with clear budgets and conversion definitions.
  3. Review search terms, qualified leads, and cost per booked job before expanding.

What to check before getting started

If you spent $1,000 to get 20 inquiries, cost per inquiry is $50. If only five met your definition of a qualified lead, cost per qualified lead is $200. Agree on what qualifies before comparing campaigns.

If you want to audit an existing Google Ads account or set up a new campaign, review Grid Rankers’ PPC management services.

Frequently asked questions

What budget should I start with?

Estimate from the services you want to advertise, local click costs, conversion rates, and the number of leads needed to learn. Set ad spend apart from agency fees.

What’s a useful metric beyond cost per lead?

Cost per qualified lead and cost per booked job show whether inexpensive inquiries are actually valuable.

Is PPC a substitute for SEO?

No. Paid search buys ad exposure while campaigns run. SEO improves the site and listings for unpaid discovery. Both can support the same business goals.
